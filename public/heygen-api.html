Break-even: HeyGen LiveAvatar vs self-hosting on RunPod

For a screening tool, HeyGen LiveAvatar is cheaper unless you're running hundreds of interviews a month. An H100 is also the wrong GPU for self-hosting this. The figures below assume 30-minute interviews.

HeyGen LiveAvatar. On the Essential plan, FULL mode works out to 20 cents per minute and LITE mode to 10 cents per minute. FULL includes HeyGen's own speech recognition, LLM and voice. In FULL mode, LiveAvatar uses OpenAI 4o-mini, which is thin for judging candidates, so you'd probably want LITE with your own LLM.

Avoid the Starter plan for this. It has up to 5 concurrent streams and a 5-minute limit per session, which would cut every interview off. Check the session limits on Essential and Business before committing.

RunPod H100. RunPod lists the H100 PCIe at $1.99 per GPU-hour and the H100 SXM at $2.69 per GPU-hour. That's about 3–4.5p per minute while it runs. The catch is that a real-time avatar has to be warm whenever a candidate turns up.

At list prices, an always-on H100 only beats HeyGen LITE above roughly 15,000 minutes a month, about 480 interviews of 30 minutes each.

Why an H100 is overkill. On RunPod you wouldn't run H3 Max. You'd run an open real-time model such as MuseTalk, which hit 30 fps and above on a V100. An RTX 4090 at $0.39/hour would likely be enough, which cuts the RunPod rows by about 6×. Even so, the cost you'd really be paying is engineering time:

  • Building and running WebRTC, speech-to-text, TTS and turn-taking yourself
  • Cold starts if you scale the GPU to zero
  • Visibly lower avatar quality than HeyGen's

Recommendation: start on HeyGen LITE with your own LLM, so your scoring logic stays in your hands. Revisit RunPod plus a 4090 only if volume heads towards several hundred interviews a month. Whichever you choose, tell candidates up front that they're speaking to an AI, and keep a human reviewing decisions. In the UK, solely automated hiring decisions run into UK GDPR rules.
